Ashutosh Ojha

AI for automated and open-ended discovery.

I’m a final-year student in the Integrated M.Tech program in Mathematics and Computing at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad, and a research intern at Amazon Lab126 in Bangalore. At Lab126, I’m building a search that discovers how to compress neural networks on its own: which subnetwork to keep, which dimensions to prune and by how much, and when to pause for recovery training.

I’m interested in using AI for automated and open-ended discovery, and in making AI systems more efficient. At Adobe MDSR, I built MEMENTO, an agent that learns both facts and search strategies from the open web without retraining the model. At IBM Research, I used disagreement between LLMs to build a taxonomy of real developer questions; it won the Best Paper Award at the IBM Research Internship Symposium and appears in Findings of EMNLP 2026. I’ve also studied how diffusion language models connect reasoning traces to their answers, and applied physics-informed neural networks to gravitational lensing over two Google Summer of Code terms with ML4SCI, where I returned as a mentor in 2026.

I graduate in May 2027 and am looking for pre-doctoral research positions. I’d be glad to hear from labs working on related problems.

    What Do Developers Ask? A Taxonomy of Code Related Questions

    Ashutosh Ojha*, Ashita Saxena*, Varshini Reddy Pateel, Palanivel Kodeswaran, Sayandeep Sen, Srikanth G. Tamilselvam

    Findings of EMNLP 2026

    Built from 3,052 real conversations with repository-level code assistants, the taxonomy has 86 question types and is refined by a loop driven by disagreement between LLMs. We use it to audit 11 CodeQA benchmarks and to guide synthetic data generation.

  2. 2026

    Trace–Answer Compatibility Emerges at Depth and Shapes Answer Prediction in Diffusion Language Models

    Ashutosh Ojha, Pranath Reddy, Sergei Gleyzer

    DiffuLM Workshop, NeurIPS 2026

    In LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B, a decoder trained without correctness labels separates original from swapped trace–answer pairs at deep blocks (AUC 0.919 and 0.897), and patching late answer-slot states recovers 66 to 86% of the trace-conditioned answer-likelihood gap.

    PDF Workshop

  3. 2026

    MEMENTO: Leveraging Web as a Learning Signal for Low-Data Domains

    Ashutosh Ojha, Vinay Aggarwal, Ashutosh Srivastava, Siddharth Yedlapati, Yaman K. Singla, Jitendra Ajmera

    Under review

    Treats the web as a learning signal rather than a stateless lookup: an adaptive exploration tree within each session, and separate memories for facts and search strategies across sessions. Improves on ReAct baselines by 25.6% on sales automation and 36.5% on legal research, with no model training.
